2. Rules of Thumb

  1. Soldering tip = melting point + 120–150 °C.
  2. Desoldering tip = soldering tip + 20–40 °C. Wick and pump steal heat, and you need the whole joint fully liquid.
  3. Bigger tip before higher temperature. A 3 mm chisel at 330 °C beats a needle tip at 400 °C.
  4. Joint time: 2–4 s. If it hasn’t flowed in 5 s, increase heat delivery (tip size → flux → +20 °C). Don’t just hold longer.
  5. Never exceed ~400 °C on a standard FR-4 board: pads lift and tips die.
  6. Too hot signs: flux burns black instantly, tip goes dark, board browns.
  7. Too cold signs: solder balls up, looks grey and lumpy, won’t spread.

3. Soldering Procedure

Through-hole

  1. Tin the tip, wipe on brass wool.
  2. Touch the tip to pad and lead together.
  3. Feed solder into the joint on the opposite side from the tip (not onto the tip).
  4. Stop feeding when you get a concave fillet covering the pad.
  5. Remove solder → remove iron 1 s later.
  6. Don’t move the part for 2–3 s (critical for 60/40).

SMD (resistors, caps)

  1. Tin one pad.
  2. Hold part with tweezers, reflow that pad to tack it.
  3. Solder the second pad, then touch up the first.

SMD ICs (SOIC/QFP)

  1. Tack two opposite corner pins, check alignment.
  2. Apply flux along all pins.
  3. Drag a solder-loaded tip along the pins.
  4. Remove bridges with flux + clean tip, or wick.

4. Desoldering Procedure

Golden rule

Add fresh solder + flux first. Old solder is oxidised and won’t flow. On lead-free boards, adding leaded solder drops the melting point and makes removal much easier (hobby/repair only, not for RoHS products).

Solder wick

  1. Flux the joint.
  2. Braid on joint, iron on top of braid.
  3. Wait until solder wicks up (copper turns silver).
  4. Lift iron and braid together. Never drag or pull.
  5. Cut used braid. Match braid width to tip width.

Solder pump

  1. Cock the pump.
  2. Melt the joint fully (add fresh solder if dry).
  3. Nozzle on the molten solder, remove iron, trigger immediately.
  4. Repeat once if needed, then wiggle the pin to confirm it’s free.

Hot air

  1. Flux all pins.
  2. Shield nearby plastic parts with Kapton tape or foil.
  3. Nozzle 1–2 cm above the part, keep it moving in small circles.
  4. Start low airflow (small parts blow away on high).
  5. Lift with tweezers only when all pins are molten. Test with a gentle nudge.
  6. Clean pads with wick before fitting the new part.

Hot air set-point ≠ temperature at the board. Air cools quickly with distance, so the set-point is higher than the melting point by a large margin.

5. Troubleshooting

Problem Cause Fix
Grey, lumpy joint Too cold / dirty Flux, reheat until it flows
Frosty, cracked joint Moved while cooling Reheat, hold still
Solder bridge Too much solder Flux + drag clean tip, or wick
Solder won’t wick Braid cold or dry Add flux, +20 °C, wider braid
Pump leaves solder in hole Joint not fully molten Add fresh solder, re-melt, retry
Pad lifted Too hot / too long / pulled Lower temp, lift braid vertically; repair with jumper wire
Tip won’t wet Oxidised tip Tip tinner/activator; never file a plated tip

6. Tip Care

  • Tin the tip before putting it in the stand and before switching off.
  • Clean on brass wool, not a soaking-wet sponge.
  • Use sleep mode. Idling at high temperature destroys tips.
  • Lead-free wears tips faster; lower the temperature whenever possible.

7. Safety

  • Wash hands after handling leaded solder; no food or drink at the bench.
  • Use fume extraction: flux smoke irritates lungs.
  • Always return the iron to the stand.
  • Wear safety glasses when cutting leads.
